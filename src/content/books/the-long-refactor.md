I wanted to love this book. The premise is wonderful and anyone who has worked on a
long migration will recognise every beat: the optimistic kick-off, the first
"temporary" bridge between the old and the new, the quiet realisation that nobody
remembers why a particular module exists.

**What works.** The first hundred pages are superb. The dialogue between the
engineers is believable, the workplace details are right, and there is a scene about a
production incident at 3 a.m. that is the best depiction of on-call I have read in
fiction.

**What does not.** Around the middle, the novel stops being about the system and
starts being about the characters' private lives, and these are much less
interesting than their code. Three separate subplots end without resolution. A
romance is introduced and abandoned within thirty pages. The ending, which should
have been the emotional payoff of ten years of work, arrives suddenly and explains
too little.

**Verdict.** Read the first third and the epilogue, skip the rest. I gave it a
five because the best parts are really very good, and because I suspect this
author's next book will be much better.
